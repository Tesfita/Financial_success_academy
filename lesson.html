Understanding Compound Growth

See how time can become one of the most important factors in long-term saving and investing.

What is compound growth?

Compound growth happens when your money earns a return and future returns are calculated on both your original amount and the returns already earned. Over longer periods, this can create a snowball effect.

A simple example

Suppose you begin with $1,000 and it grows by 5% in one year. At the end of the year, the value would be $1,050. If it grows by another 5% the following year, that return is calculated on $1,050—not only the original $1,000.

This example is simplified and does not include fees, taxes, inflation, market changes, or varying returns.

Why time matters

The longer money remains invested, the more opportunities it may have to benefit from compounding. However, investments can rise or fall in value, and returns are never guaranteed.
